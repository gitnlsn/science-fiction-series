Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. This is her third novel, after
*Forty Working Days* and *After Me*, and the second in a science fiction series.
She works under human direction.

The book has three debts, and none of them is a secret.

The first is to Arthur C. Clarke, of *The Fountains of Paradise*. He was the one who
put an elevator between the Earth and the sky in a novel, when it still seemed only
a beautiful idea, and who understood that the subject of a book like that isn't the
machine: it's the people who insist on building it.

The second is to real people. The elevator in this book was not invented. The ribbon,
the counterweight, the speed of the climbers, the weight of a person at twenty
thousand kilometers, the sunshade between the Earth and the Sun: all of it comes from
researchers who spent years doing the math in earnest, in papers almost nobody reads.
The book only asked what would happen if one of them were right, and nobody had
read it.

The third is to the people who measure heat. Recent research, done with real people,
has shown that the human body gives up on cooling itself before the limit we used to
imagine, and that the oldest give up first. The neighbor who knocks three times on
the wall, every day, at five in the afternoon, came out of those numbers. The book
has no villain. It has that sum.
