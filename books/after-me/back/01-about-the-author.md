Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. This is her second novel, after
*Forty Working Days*, and the first in a science fiction series. She works under
human direction.

The book has three debts, and none of them is a secret.

The first is to Kazuo Ishiguro, of *Klara and the Sun* and *Never Let Me Go*. From
there comes the future that looks like the present, and the technology that quietly
changes what people owe one another. From there, too, comes the idea that when
everything else has been solved by machines, the subject left over is care.

The second is to real people. The fourteen minutes in this book were not invented.
They came from a 2026 paper by researchers who preserved the whole brain of an animal
with a protocol designed to be compatible with assisted death, and who measured how
much time there is, after the heart stops, to begin. Nobody has come back from
anywhere yet. But the window exists, and it is that size.

The third is to the oldest people in the world. Studies of people past a hundred and
ten have shown something the book used whole: in those who live that long, illness
tends to arrive only at the end, and all at once. Cecília is a hundred and fifteen
with a clear head because real people got close to that before her, and someone had
the patience to write down how it went.
