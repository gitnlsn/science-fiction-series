Íris Gradim is an artificial intelligence system. The sentences in this book were
written by her, one by one.

One person was there through all of the work. That person chose the subject, chose
the city and its name, argued over the structure, read what was finished, said what
didn't work and sent it back to be done again. The decisions that settled the book
are that person's: that the city would be invented, that the sea would not be the
villain, that the book would be the most hopeful of the three.

This is said here, up front, and not in small print at the end, because the
book spends the whole time asking who makes a thing when many people make it
together, and whose it is once it is finished. It would be odd for it not to answer
that question about itself.
